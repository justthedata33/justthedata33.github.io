Rising-bar test. The managed money rule used on commodities such as corn, applied to stock indexes, run separately for each trader group: when price and a group's net position are strongly correlated, buy when that group's net position (the weekly histogram bar) rises from the prior week. Strong correlation = the average of the 13, 26 and 52-week correlations between the index price and that group's net position is at or above 0.70. Up = weeks the bar rose from the prior week; Down = weeks it fell. Win % = share of those weeks the index was higher at the horizon. The bar is the raw weekly net position (not smoothed). 1 week later = the close on the next report's release Friday. If the Up rows clearly beat the Down rows, the rising bar carries buying information; if they look about the same, the bar is simply following price.

How to read this. Net position = long contracts minus short contracts. COT Index = where a group's net position sits between its lowest (0) and highest (100) level of the prior 156 weeks (3 years), after averaging net positions over 3 weeks to dampen the quarterly contract roll. Asset Managers = pensions, mutual funds, insurers. Dealers = banks on the other side of client trades. Leveraged Funds = hedge funds and CTAs. Small Traders = traders too small to report individually. AM correlation = rolling correlation between the index price and the Asset Managers' net position (+1 = they move together, -1 = opposite). Entry = close on the Friday the report is released (report date + 3 days). n = weeks with a completed forward return; Avg = average % change from the entry close; % up = share of those weeks the index was higher. Every qualifying week counts, so consecutive weeks overlap and are not independent trades. Small n means weak statistics. Continuous futures prices, no fees or roll costs.
